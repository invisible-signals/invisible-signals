# Onsite

## Goal of this phase

Land the job. The onsite is the most comprehensive stage — multiple interviewers, multiple formats, signals being read simultaneously across technical ability, communication, collaboration, and judgment.

## Audience

A panel of interviewers across functions: engineers, tech leads, product managers, your potential manager, sometimes a skip-level. Each is evaluating a different dimension. The same story needs to land differently depending on who is in the room.

## What the reviewer is looking for

- Technical ability under structured conditions (whiteboarding, system design, code review, debugging)
- Behavioral fit through past experience — how you have handled real situations
- Communication and self-awareness
- How you handle ambiguity, pressure, and problems you have not seen before
- Whether they would want to work with you

## Visible signals

- Performance on technical exercises
- STAR story quality — specificity, ownership, and outcome clarity
- How you adapt your communication to different interviewers
- Whether you ask genuine questions at the end of each round

## Invisible signals

- First impressions form fast — 30% of interviewers make a hiring decision within the first 15 minutes
- How you treat everyone in the room, not just the senior people
- Whether your behavioral examples reveal ownership or diffuse it
- Energy and presence — are you engaged or are you just enduring the process?

## Common mistakes

- Using the same answer regardless of which interviewer is asking
- Behavioral stories that describe team outcomes without naming your individual contribution
- Not preparing STAR stories in advance — improvised stories are usually vague
- Going silent during technical exercises instead of narrating your thinking
- Not asking questions at the end of rounds — it reads as disengagement

## How to stand out

Tailor responses to the interviewer's role:

- **Engineer** — how you think technically, your process, what you have built
- **Product manager** — how you reason about impact and tradeoffs
- **Manager** — how you operate on a team, handle conflict, give and receive feedback

Prepare three to five STAR stories before the onsite. Choose stories that cover different signals — technical depth, collaboration, ownership, judgment under pressure. Know them well enough to tell conversationally, not recited.

**STAR method:**

| Component | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Situation** | The context and details of the example |
| **Task** | Your specific responsibility |
| **Action** | The steps you took |
| **Result** | The outcome of your actions |

Prompt to practice: *"Tell me about a time when..."*

## AI-assisted prep

- Generate a list of likely behavioral questions for this role and level
- Draft STAR stories from your experience notes and identify where ownership language is weak
- Practice tailoring the same story for different interviewer types (engineer vs. PM vs. manager)
- Run a mock whiteboard or system design session and ask for feedback on reasoning clarity
