# Technical Screen

## Goal of this phase

Highlight your technical skills. The interviewer is evaluating depth — not just whether you know the answer, but whether you understand tradeoffs and can reason through problems you have not seen before.

## Audience

Senior engineer or tech lead on the hiring team. They are not looking for a perfect score. They are looking for fluency, intellectual honesty, and evidence that you can do the actual work.

## What the reviewer is looking for

- Technical knowledge relevant to this specific role
- Problem-solving process — how you think, not just what you produce
- Ability to discuss real prior technical work with specificity
- Whether you ask questions that signal genuine technical curiosity

## Visible signals

- Answers to technical questions (algorithms, system design, debugging, architecture)
- How you approach a problem you have not seen before
- Technical vocabulary and how accurately you use it
- The quality of questions you ask about the team's stack and challenges

## Invisible signals

- Whether you narrate your reasoning or just produce answers silently
- How you handle the edges of your knowledge — do you bluff or acknowledge limits honestly?
- Whether your knowledge connects to real work or feels surface-level
- Intellectual curiosity — are you interested in the problem, or just trying to get through the interview?

## Common mistakes

- Claiming deep knowledge in areas where you only have exposure
- Going silent while solving — the interviewer cannot see your reasoning if you do not narrate it
- Studying breadth (memorizing interview lists) instead of depth in areas relevant to the role
- Not preparing questions about the team's technical environment
- Overconfidence in areas that the interview then exposes as shallow

## How to stand out

- Narrate your reasoning — say what you are considering before committing to an approach
- Connect your answers to real work: *"I ran into something similar when we had to..."*
- Acknowledge the limits of your knowledge clearly, then reason from what you do know
- Ask real technical questions: What does the team's architecture look like? Where is the most complexity right now? What does the debugging process look like when something goes wrong in production?

Ways to stay sharp before the interview:

- Attend local tech events and conference talks in your domain
- Build or contribute to sample projects
- Work through platform-specific tutorials and codelabs

## AI-assisted prep

- Practice explaining a technical concept out loud and ask AI to identify gaps or unclear reasoning
- Generate likely technical questions for this role and domain
- Review a past technical contribution and prepare to discuss the decisions made and why
- Identify the most relevant technical areas from the job description and create a targeted study list
