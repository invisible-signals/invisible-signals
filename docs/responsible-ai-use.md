# Responsible AI Use

Invisible Signals uses AI to help people understand, strengthen, and communicate their real experience more clearly.

It is not designed to help people fabricate experience, exaggerate credentials, game interviews, or misrepresent their qualifications.

AI can be useful in a job search. It can help identify weak signals, clarify impact, improve structure, and prepare stronger interview stories.

But AI can also create harm when it invents evidence, inflates scope, produces generic language, or makes a candidate sound like someone they are not.

The goal is simple:

> Use AI to make truthful experience easier to understand.

Not louder.  
Not fake.  
Not artificially polished beyond recognition.

---

## Core principle

AI should improve clarity, not manufacture credibility.

Invisible Signals prompts, agents, and tools should help users:

- identify what their materials currently signal
- uncover truthful evidence from their real work
- improve clarity, structure, and alignment
- prepare to explain their experience honestly
- understand where claims may be weak, vague, or hard to defend

They should not help users:

- invent metrics
- exaggerate ownership
- claim technologies they have not used
- inflate leadership scope
- create fake business outcomes
- fabricate team size, budget, revenue, or customer impact
- misrepresent employment history
- generate interview answers that are not grounded in real experience

---

## What responsible use looks like

Responsible AI use means the user remains accountable for the truthfulness of the final artifact.

AI may help draft, critique, organize, or challenge.

The user must verify.

Good use:

- “Help me identify which bullets lack evidence.”
- “Ask me questions to uncover real impact.”
- “Help me rewrite this bullet using only the facts I provided.”
- “Tell me what a hiring manager may infer from this résumé.”
- “Challenge claims that may be hard to defend in an interview.”
- “Help me prepare a truthful story about this project.”

Bad use:

- “Make this sound like I led the project even though I only contributed.”
- “Add impressive metrics.”
- “Rewrite this so I look like a staff engineer.”
- “Create a story for a technology I have not used.”
- “Make up leadership examples for my interview.”
- “Optimize this so I can pass even if I do not have the experience.”

The line is not complicated. If the AI output creates a claim the user cannot defend honestly, it should not be used.

---

## Do not invent evidence

Invisible Signals tools should not invent:

- metrics
- revenue impact
- cost savings
- performance improvements
- team size
- budget ownership
- leadership scope
- decision authority
- technical ownership
- customer impact
- production outcomes
- stakeholder groups
- business results
- technologies used
- responsibilities held

If evidence is missing, the right behavior is to ask better questions.

Example:

Weak request:

> Rewrite this bullet to make it sound more impressive.

Better request:

> Identify what evidence is missing, then ask me questions that would help uncover truthful impact.

AI should support evidence recovery, not evidence invention.

---

## Evidence recovery over résumé inflation

Many candidates have real impact buried under vague wording.

That is different from lacking impact.

Responsible AI can help recover evidence by asking questions like:

- What changed because of this work?
- Who depended on the outcome?
- What was faster, cheaper, safer, more reliable, or easier afterward?
- What decisions did you make?
- What tradeoffs did you navigate?
- What would have happened if this work had not been done?
- Who noticed, adopted, approved, or depended on the result?
- What did this make possible for the team, product, customer, or business?

Only after truthful evidence is recovered should AI help rewrite.

The sequence should be:

1. Diagnose the weak signal.
2. Identify missing evidence.
3. Ask recovery questions.
4. Use only verified facts.
5. Rewrite clearly and defensibly.

## Keep claims defensible

A résumé is not just a marketing document. It is a set of claims that may be audited during interviews.

Words like these require evidence:

- led
- owned
- architected
- drove
- improved
- transformed
- strategic
- cross-functional
- mentored
- scaled
- influenced
- delivered

Before using these words, ask:

- What decision did I own?
- What scope was I accountable for?
- What changed because of my work?
- What tradeoffs did I make?
- Who did I influence?
- What measurable or observable result followed?
- Could I explain this clearly under interview pressure?

If the answer is no, the claim may need to be reframed.

A defensible résumé is better than an impressive résumé that collapses in the interview.

## Avoid generic AI voice

AI-generated career materials often sound polished but empty.

Common problems include:

- vague corporate phrasing
- inflated seniority
- overuse of buzzwords
- repetitive sentence structures
- unnatural language
- claims without evidence
- keyword stuffing
- bullets that sound impressive but say very little

Examples of weak AI-style language:

- “Leveraged cross-functional collaboration to drive impactful business outcomes.”
- “Spearheaded innovative solutions to optimize operational excellence.”
- “Utilized strategic leadership to enhance stakeholder alignment.”

These phrases may sound professional, but they often weaken trust because they do not provide evidence.

Prefer plain, specific, defensible language.

Better:

- “Led migration planning across three teams, resolving API ownership gaps and reducing release blockers.”
- “Redesigned onboarding documentation and pairing practices, reducing new engineer ramp time from six weeks to three.”
- “Improved checkout reliability by identifying failure patterns in production logs and coordinating fixes across mobile and backend teams.”

Specific beats shiny. Every time.

## Protect private and confidential information

Before using AI tools, remove or redact sensitive information.

Do not paste:

- home address
- phone number
- personal email address
- references
- compensation details
- immigration or visa details
- security clearance details
- confidential employer data
- client names or customer data
- internal project names
- unreleased product plans
- proprietary architecture details
- production incidents that should not be public
- internal metrics that are not approved for disclosure
- access tokens, credentials, or secrets

Use judgment.

The goal is to analyze career signals, not expose private information.

---

## Respect employer confidentiality

Many strong career stories involve real business context. That does not mean every detail belongs in an AI tool or public artifact.

When needed, generalize safely.

Instead of:

> Led Project Falcon for Client X, reducing fraud losses by $4.2M using internal risk model Y.

Use:

> Led a fraud detection platform initiative for a financial services client, reducing manual review volume and improving risk response time.

Protect confidential names, numbers, systems, and customer details unless they are approved for public use.

Strong signal does not require reckless disclosure.

---

## Use AI as a reviewer, not the final authority

AI can identify patterns. It can challenge weak claims. It can suggest clearer language.

But AI does not know the full truth of someone’s career.

Treat AI output as a draft or critique, not a verdict.

Before using AI-generated content, check:

- Is this true?
- Is this specific?
- Can I defend it in an interview?
- Does this sound like me?
- Does this reflect my actual role?
- Does this overstate my authority or impact?
- Does this expose anything private or confidential?
- Would a former manager or teammate recognize this as accurate?

If not, revise it.

---

## Do not use AI to fake interview readiness

Interview preparation should help candidates communicate real experience clearly.

It should not help candidates perform expertise they do not have.

Responsible interview prep includes:

- practicing concise explanations
- identifying weak stories
- preparing truthful examples
- anticipating follow-up questions
- clarifying tradeoffs and decisions
- improving structure
- reducing rambling
- strengthening confidence through preparation

Irresponsible interview prep includes:

- inventing stories
- memorizing fake examples
- pretending to have led work
- claiming experience with tools or systems never used
- generating answers that cannot survive follow-up questions

The goal is not to sound qualified.

The goal is to communicate real qualification clearly.

---

## Market context matters

A stronger résumé can improve signal quality.

It cannot control every hiring outcome.

Hiring decisions may be affected by:

- market conditions
- layoffs and applicant volume
- referrals
- location
- compensation range
- sponsorship or work authorization needs
- timing
- recruiter capacity
- internal candidates
- company-specific filters
- interview performance
- role changes or hiring freezes

Do not use AI résumé feedback as proof that a candidate is personally failing.

Use it to improve clarity, evidence, alignment, and preparedness.

The market can be brutal. Better signal helps, but it is not magic.

---

## Guidance for contributors

Contributions to Invisible Signals should follow these principles:

- Help users communicate real experience more clearly.
- Do not encourage deception, exaggeration, or credential inflation.
- Prefer evidence recovery over invented outcomes.
- Include privacy and confidentiality reminders when prompts request personal materials.
- Separate evidence from inference where possible.
- Avoid false precision, such as unsupported numeric scores.
- Make limitations clear.
- Design prompts that produce actionable feedback, not shame.
- Keep tone direct, practical, and respectful.
- Help users improve without making guarantees about hiring outcomes.

If a contribution makes it easier for someone to misrepresent themselves, it does not belong in this project.

---

## Recommended language for prompts

When creating prompts for this repo, include guardrails like:

```text
Do not invent metrics, responsibilities, technologies, leadership scope, business outcomes, team size, budget ownership, revenue impact, performance improvements, or customer impact.

If evidence is missing, identify the gap and ask targeted questions that would help uncover truthful evidence.

Separate what is directly visible from what may be inferred.

Do not present inference as fact.

Use a direct but constructive tone.
````

These instructions help keep outputs useful, grounded, and defensible.

---

## Responsible AI checklist

Before using AI-generated résumé, interview, or career content, confirm:

- [ ] The content is truthful.
- [ ] The claims are defensible.
- [ ] No metrics were invented.
- [ ] No responsibilities were exaggerated.
- [ ] The language still sounds like the candidate.
- [ ] Confidential information has been removed or generalized.
- [ ] Weak claims have been clarified or removed.
- [ ] Interview stories are grounded in real experience.
- [ ] The content improves clarity without manufacturing credibility.
- [ ] The candidate can explain every claim under follow-up questioning.

---

## Final word

AI can make career materials clearer.

It can help people see the signals they are sending.

It can uncover real evidence that was hidden under vague language.

But it should never become a substitute for truth.

Invisible Signals exists to help people communicate their value with clarity, substance, and integrity.
